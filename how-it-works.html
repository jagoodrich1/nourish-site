How the answers work

Updated 18 November 2026

Allergens and diets

We read the words on a menu. If a dish names something, like shrimp or cheese, we say it contains it. A dish that names nothing is shown as not listed, which is not the same as free of it, so ask.

We only say a dish is free of something in two cases: the menu says so itself, or it is a sealed product whose label the law requires to declare the major allergens. A menu calling a dish gluten free or vegan is the restaurant’s word about its recipe, not about what else its kitchen cooks.

Some filters ask about one thing on a dish rather than all of it. Organic, grass fed and locally sourced mean the menu says it about something on the dish.

The percent on a restaurant

It is the share of what the menu answers that fits what you avoid, counting meals and leaving out drinks and sides. If a menu says nothing about most of its dishes for what you avoid, we do not show a percent at all, because it would rest on too little.

The order of the Map list

Best match puts first the places where a meal fits what you need, and among those the highest percent. A card with a percent comes before one without. After that, a score that also weighs health and distance decides, and where several branches of one chain would sit side by side they are spread out.

Closest is nearest first. Healthiest puts the highest health score first, among places where a meal fits. Estimates are labelled, and when we cannot confirm something, we say so.

Health ratings

A rating only appears where a restaurant publishes nutrition, which mostly means chains. With no published numbers there is no rating, and the app says so.

Where a dish has a published weight, it is scored with the UK Food Standards Agency’s Nutrient Profiling Model, per 100 g, which weighs energy, saturated fat, sugars and sodium against fruit and vegetables, fibre and protein. Where it has no weight, it is scored against energy, using the Pan American Health Organization’s limits for sodium, fats and sugars and the EU’s thresholds for protein and fibre. A restaurant’s rating comes from its dishes. The 1 to 10 scale itself is ours.

Estimates

A menu we hold shows only the figures the restaurant published. A chain’s older rows, a photo you scan and a food you search for can show an estimate, marked as one, and it can be far off. A rating is about the mix of nutrients and not about you.

None of this is medical advice. Talk to a doctor or dietitian about your own health.
